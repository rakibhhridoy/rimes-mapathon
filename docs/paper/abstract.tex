Flood susceptibility maps for Bangladesh commonly report areas under the ROC curve (AUC) near or above 0.9, yet most are trained on a few hundred inventory points and tested on locations beside the ones they learned from. This study trains flood models for three regions of Bangladesh on flood extents mapped from Sentinel-1 radar in thirteen past events, and tests every model on 10\,km blocks held out from training, repeated over \RRBenchSeeds{} block assignments. A gradient-boosted tree scoring every asset mapped in OpenStreetMap reaches a mean AUC of \RRBenchBoostingAUC{} in Rangpur and Rajshahi, \SylhetBenchBoostingAUC{} in Sylhet and \CoastalBenchBoostingAUC{} on the south-west coast. Training on the observed extents rather than on terrain-threshold labels raises the AUC by \RRLabelGap{} in Rangpur and Rajshahi and \SylhetLabelGap{} in Sylhet, on every block assignment. A graph neural network linking each asset to its neighbours trailed the tree by \RRBenchGap{} and \SylhetBenchGap{} in the two riverine regions and tied with it on the coast, so the graph added nothing that the features did not already carry. On the coast the terrain hazard surface is no better than chance against observed flooding, because surge flooding follows embankments and tides that elevation does not describe. \pending{one sentence on transfer to the 2024 floods.} A landslide model for the Chittagong Hill Tracts, fitted to \CHTLandslides{} landslides mapped after a single storm, reaches an AUC of \CHTLSAUC{}. Hazard, exposure and vulnerability are combined by geometric mean on a 500\,m grid, and the resulting ranking of cells holds under most alternative weightings. The pipeline, data and a public dashboard are released openly.
