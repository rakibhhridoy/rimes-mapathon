Flood susceptibility maps for Bangladesh commonly report areas under the ROC curve (AUC) near or above 0.9, yet most are trained on a few hundred to a few thousand sampled points and tested beside the locations they learned from. This study trains flood models for three regions on extents mapped from Sentinel-1 radar in thirteen past events and tests them on 10\,km blocks held out from training, over \RRBenchSeeds{} block assignments. A gradient-boosted tree scoring every asset mapped in OpenStreetMap reaches a mean AUC of \RRBenchBoostingAUC{} in Rangpur and Rajshahi, \SylhetBenchBoostingAUC{} in Sylhet and \CoastalBenchBoostingAUC{} on the south-west coast. Training on the observed extents rather than on terrain-threshold labels raises the AUC by \RRLabelGap{}, \SylhetLabelGap{} and \CoastalLabelGap{}, and a graph neural network linking neighbouring assets trails the best tabular model in every region, measurably so in the riverine ones. Trained only on floods up to 2022, the model ranks the ground the 2024 floods reached at \RRTempAUC{}, \SylhetTempAUC{} and \CoastalTempAUC{}. In the riverine regions the record of earlier flooding ranks it better, at \RRTempPastAUC{} and \SylhetTempPastAUC{}, yet a model given that record as a feature beats both, at \RRPastFeatAUC{} and \SylhetPastFeatAUC{}, because terrain ranks the ground no earlier flood reached. On the coast the terrain hazard surface is close to chance, because surge flooding follows embankments and tides that elevation does not describe. A landslide model for the Chittagong Hill Tracts, fitted to \CHTLandslides{} landslides from one storm, reaches an AUC of \CHTLSAUC{}. Hazard, exposure and vulnerability are combined on a 500\,m grid, and the pipeline, data and a public dashboard are released openly.
