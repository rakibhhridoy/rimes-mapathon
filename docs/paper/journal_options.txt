JOURNAL OPTIONS FOR THE FLOOD PAPER
"Do flood susceptibility models know more than the flood record? Evidence
from radar-observed floods in Bangladesh"

Compiled 2026-09-26 from web searches.

REQUIREMENTS (from the authors, 2026-09-26)
  - Q1 or Q2, with a Web of Science Journal Impact Factor
  - normal timeline (3-6 months) is fine
  - an open-access fee of USD 1,500-3,000 can be paid if no waiver comes through
  - readership: flood risk
  - priority: acceptance with few resubmissions, not prestige

CAUTION ON THE NUMBERS
  Official Clarivate impact factors could not be reached (Springer and Wiley
  block automated access; NHESS does not publish its impact factor). Most
  figures below come from secondary metric sites, which often disagree with
  each other. Marked [publisher] where the figure came from the publisher's
  own page or announcement. CONFIRM EVERY IMPACT FACTOR AND QUARTILE IN
  JOURNAL CITATION REPORTS (via the university) BEFORE SUBMITTING, and check
  indexing on the Clarivate Master Journal List: https://mjl.clarivate.com
  No acceptance rates were found for any journal; the ease ranking is a
  judgement of fit and editorial culture, not a statistic.


RECOMMENDATION
  Two strategies:
    Ease first:  Scientific Reports. The easiest route to a Q1 journal with an
                 impact factor, because it reviews for soundness only, not
                 novelty or significance.
    Fit first:   Journal of Flood Risk Management. Flood-risk reviewers are
                 the ones most likely to value the validation argument.
  Fallback chain either way:
    Stochastic Environmental Research and Risk Assessment
    -> Natural Hazards (both free by the subscription route)
    -> Remote Sensing (MDPI)
  Reach higher:  NHESS, accepting a harder, public review.
